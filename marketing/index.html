The workflow

Inbox, but shaped like real work.

Three ideas from the HEY inbox, built into a desktop app you can drop into your existing email account in under two minutes.

The Gate

First-time senders land in a separate screener, not in your inbox. Approve who gets through. Block the rest. Per-message, or in a Tinder-style batch. The history view keeps every decision you ever made.

G Y N

The Imbox

New for you on top, Previously seen below. Senders with three or more unread collapse into a single bundle. A read-together loop walks you through everything unread in order.

J K O

The Piles

Reply Later, Set Aside, Bubble Up. Stack mail by what you intend to do with it. Stick yellow notes on threads. Save text snippets for later. Set a follow-up and SendPalm re-surfaces the message when the time comes.

Privacy

Local by architecture,
not by promise.

There is no SendPalm server. Your mail lives in a SQLite file on your machine. Your password lives in the macOS Keychain. There is nothing to leak because there is nothing stored anywhere we control.

  1. 01

    Your mail is in a local SQLite file

    A single file on your Mac, at a path the OS sandbox already protects. Back it up with Time Machine. Inspect it with sqlite3. Move it between machines like any other document.

  2. 02

    Your IMAP password is in the macOS Keychain

    Not in the app's preferences. Not in the database. Not in any file you might accidentally share. The Keychain is gated by your user account and the Tauri sandbox.

  3. 03

    There is no SendPalm sync service

    Each device is its own SQLite. Your laptop and your iPhone stay in sync only because IMAP is the source of truth for the actual mail. Sticky notes, piles, and labels are local, and that is documented, not glossed over.
